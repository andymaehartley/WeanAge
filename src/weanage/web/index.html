    One row per animal, as CSV or Excel. Required columns: specimen_id, site, species, d15N. Young animals also need an age at death in months: age_months, or a range as age_min_months + age_max_months . Mark adults with age_class = adult and sex = F for the mothers.

    One row per dentine increment, as CSV or Excel. Required columns: specimen_id, site, species, tooth, position_mm, d15N . Optional: individual_id (several teeth from one animal), position_reference + crown_height_mm (if measured from the cervix).

    How the bone calculation works

    Bone collagen is renewed slowly, so a young animal's bone δ¹⁵N mixes everything it ate up to death. A single bone cannot show when that animal was weaned. Many animals that died at different ages can: together they trace how δ¹⁵N rises while suckling and falls after weaning.

    1. Diet. Milk until weaning starts; then other food takes over, slowly at first, until weaning ends.
    2. Bone. Born with the mother's value; each month part of the bone is rebuilt from the current diet, faster while the skeleton grows.
    3. Fit. The weaning ages, the nursing enrichment and the weaning-food δ¹⁵N are estimated together from all the animals at a site (Bayesian sampler).

    After WARN (Tsutaya & Yoneda 2013), which was built for human skeletons. Bone turnover rates for livestock are placeholders (TBD-LAB) until they are measured.

      Time averaging

      Each 1 mm section mixes dentine formed over weeks, and oblique growth layers add later-formed dentine to every section (Tsutaya 2020; MDSS). With this on, each sample is fitted as the average of the curve across its age window.

      Seasonality

      Elbow method (WEAN-style)

      Adult-female baseline

      Mean δ¹⁵N of adult females (the mothers' population) at each site, as used by WARN. Used to report nursing enrichment and, optionally, to hold the adult plateau near this value. Leave blank if unknown.

      Age models (tooth growth)

      Convert position along the tooth into age at formation. Edit a table's points, calibrate one from known-age reference data, or switch to a simple linear model. Saved tables are stored in your user folder and used every time.

              About WeanAge

              WeanAge estimates the age at which young animals were weaned from δ15N measured in slices of tooth dentine. It was written for zooarchaeology: cattle, pigs and horses, and any species with a tooth growth table. Version .

              Tooth terms used in WeanAge
              
      
    
              
    
              
      
      
              
        
              Cusp apex
              The tip of the crown. Dentine starts forming here, so it is the zero point: position_mm = 0.
              
        
              Crown
              The part covered by enamel. Livestock molars have tall crowns; a cattle M2 crown records roughly the first year of life.
              
        
              Cervix (neck)
              Where the crown meets the root. If you measure from here, set position_reference = cervix and give crown_height_mm (apex to cervix).
              
        
              Root
              Dentine keeps forming in the root after the crown is complete. Samples beyond a growth table are flagged as extrapolated.
              
        
              Enamel
              The hard outer layer. It holds almost no collagen, so it is not used for δ15N.
              
        
              Dentine
              The collagen-rich tissue that is sampled. It is laid down from the cusp towards the root and is not remodelled afterwards, so each slice keeps the diet of the time it formed.
              
        
              Growth layers
              Dentine forms in thin layers that slant across the tooth, so a horizontal slice also holds some later dentine: growth_layer_offset_mm.
              
        
              Pulp cavity
              The space in the middle. The dentine next to it formed last, and there is less of it: inner_dentine_weight.
              
        
              Slice (increment)
              One sample of dentine: its middle is at position_mm and its height is increment_width_mm.
              
        
              Tooth codes
              M1, M2, M3 are the first, second and third molars (P premolar, I incisor). Each tooth needs its own growth table.
